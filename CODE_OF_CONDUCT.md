# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be civil, argue about the work, and assume the person on the other side is
trying to get the law right. That is nearly all of it.

## Why this file exists in this project

Molao asks universities, law societies, LII operators and bar councils to run
nodes and hold signing keys. An institution deciding whether to put its name to
this needs to know the project has a stated standard and a way to enforce it.
This document is that, and it is deliberately short.

## The standard

**Expected:**

- Disagree with the argument, the code, or the evidence.
- Say when you are uncertain. "I could not verify this" is a contribution.
- Accept correction without treating it as an attack, and give correction
  without making it one.
- Respect that contributors work across many jurisdictions, legal traditions and
  first languages. A convention obvious in one is not obvious in another.

**Not accepted:**

- Personal attacks, harassment, or sustained disruption.
- Demeaning comments about who someone is rather than what they argued —
  including about their jurisdiction, institution, or level of legal training.
- Publishing anyone's private information.
- Deliberately misrepresenting the project's status to a third party. Molao's
  honesty about what it has not built is load-bearing; overstating it to an
  institution damages every other contributor's credibility, and is treated as
  a conduct matter rather than a documentation one.

## Scope

Applies in the repository, in issues and pull requests, and when representing
the project to an outside body — a court, an LII, a faculty, a funder.

## Reporting

Report to the maintainers by opening a
[private security advisory](https://github.com/vul-os/molao/security/advisories/new)
— the same private channel [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md) uses, chosen here because
it is the one non-public route this project actually has. Say at the top that
the report is a conduct matter, not a vulnerability.

Reports are read by the maintainers. If a report concerns a maintainer, say so;
it will be handled by the others. Expect an acknowledgement within a week.

Do not open a public issue about a conduct matter involving a named person.

## What happens next

The maintainers will look into it and respond in proportion: a private word, a
public correction, a request to step back from a thread, or removal from the
project. Reasons are given to the person concerned. Details are not published,
because a conduct process conducted in public is a punishment rather than a
process.
